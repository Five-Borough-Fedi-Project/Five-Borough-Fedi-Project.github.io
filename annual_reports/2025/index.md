This report summarizes the activity of the Five Borough Fedi Project (5BFP) during calendar year 2025, including a high-level accounting of the organization’s financial income and expenditures. As a public-service organization that many people entrust with their time, attention, and money, 5BFP presents this report in an effort to show how it has applied its community’s investments over the past calendar year.

5BFP president Jason McIntosh (@jmac) wrote and published this report in November 2026, based on 5BFP’s financial records and other internal sources. All monetary amounts that appear in this report are expressed in U.S. dollars.

## Overall summary

5BFP saw its first full calendar year of operation in 2025, after coming online as a new nonprofit in April of the previous year. The numbers in the 2025 report are generally larger than those in the 2024 report due to the full January-to-December time period involved.

During 2025, 5BFP spent a bit less than $3,400, primarily on the hosting services that power Masto.NYC. As in 2024, 5BFP operations have continued to be largely covered by a generous corporate matching program, associated with a small number of donors. This plus small gifts from individual donors let 5BFP end 2025 with a tidy surplus of several thousand dollars in its bank account.

## Program activity

In 2025, the only public program of 5BFP was the care, maintenance, and promotion of the Masto.NYC Mastodon server. The server stayed online and healthy all year, with no significant outages or incidents to report. The Masto.NYC team, led by Sean (@seano), continues to keep the underlying software up to date with new releases from the core Mastodon software project.

In December 2025, the program welcomed George Bargoud (@gbargoud) as a new moderator of Masto.NYC, tapping him for the role after a long period of enthusiastic volunteer work as an ordinary user of the service.

## Financial summary

We took in $8,508.12 and spent $3,383.08, for a net gain of $5,125.04. Added to the $2,721.29 that 5BFP had at the end of 2024, this leaves $7,846.33 in its bank account at the end of 2025.

### Income

5BFP took in $8,508.12 during 2025:

* $7,715.50 came from a corporate charity program, with employees of that corporation using their philanthropy employment benefit to direct donations to 5BFP.

* $792.62 came from individual donors.

Individual donations grew by 84% compared to 2024. Corporate giving grew faster, more than doubling, so individual donations slipped from 10% to 9% of our total income.

### Expenditures

5BFP spent $3,383.08 during 2025:

* $2,973.51 paid for hosting and digital infrastructure:

    * $2,231.70 to Digital Ocean, the primary host of Masto.NYC services.
    * $741.81 to Cloudflare, which Masto.NYC uses for network resiliency.

* $399.57 paid for physical printing of stickers and other promotional materials.

* $10 covered bank fees.

The increase in hosting costs over 2024 reflects a full year of Digital Ocean service, since Masto.NYC migrated to a cloud-based system only in August 2024.

The organization spent no money in 2025 on email or domain registration, because our 2024 payments for these covered multiple years.

## Goals met and set

Our 2024 report projected an expenditure of "more than $3,000" on infrastructure. This estimate ended up too large by about twenty-five dollars. We project our 2026 income and expenses to largely resemble what they did in 2025.

Despite stated goals from 2024, 5BFP put only modest effort into fundraising in 2025. Admittedly, this matches the organization's ongoing modest expenses, which our at-present reliable corporate sponsorship covers single-handed. Diversifying our funding sources remains an aspirational goal.

Masto.NYC had around 180 monthly active users (MAU) by the end of 2025. While an increase over last year's 150 MAU, this falls short of our goal of 200 MAU by year's end. We retain 200 MAU as a goal to meet in 2026.
